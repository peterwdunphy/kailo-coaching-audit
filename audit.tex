\section*{Defining Product Goals}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Defining Product Goals}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Team-native, coach-facing training platform.} The team is the
    unit; the coach is the primary user.
  \item \textbf{Stronger analytical tools and coach customization} than
    TrainingPeaks or FinalSurge --- the analytics and the ability to tailor the
    surface are the differentiators, not feature parity.
  \item \textbf{A coach-determined path for athletes to interact} that
    \emph{reduces} the need for athlete buy-in. The less work an athlete has to
    do, the better the product: participation should be near-passive by default,
    with the coach deciding how much the athlete is asked to do.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Next Steps (ordered)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Restructure the hub into the five-tab layout} of
    \S\ref{sec:proposal} --- Today, Upcoming, Team, Recruiting (v2), Settings
    --- with the athlete page reached through the Team tab (roster, results,
    or the PR / progression boards) rather than as its own tab.
  \item \textbf{Athlete objects with histories and attributes.} Each athlete
    carries a history (race and training paces over time) \emph{and} a set of
    learned attributes --- e.g.\ ``performs better on hilly courses,''
    ``degrades more in rain / heat.'' These attributes are estimated from data,
    they change as more data arrives, and the set will grow. The data platform
    (\S\ref{sec:platform}) exists to populate and keep refreshing them.
  \item \textbf{Text-to-structured-workout parsing.} The planner grid must turn
    ``2 mile wu, 5 tempo, 3 cd'' into a structured workout that can be analyzed
    post hoc; the current parser misreads such inputs (see the Planner and
    Workout planner audits).
  \item \textbf{Weather upgrade.} Move the adjustment metric from dew point to
    wet-bulb globe temperature, and show conditions for four windows --- this
    morning / afternoon, next morning / afternoon --- to cover doubles.
  \item \textbf{Data \& enrichment platform build-out} (\S\ref{sec:platform}):
    divest from Athletic.net; per-race time, weather, and hill enrichment
    feeding the learned attributes.
  \item \textbf{Recruiting} --- a \emph{v2 product}. Reserved in the tab
    structure but not built in v1.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proposed structure: five main tabs}
\label{sec:proposal}

Collapse the sprawl into five tabs. This is a first pass, assembled from the
per-page recommendations above.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Today} --- the landing page. Keeps what the current Today page
    gets right (the plan, with the print option easy to reach) and adds what
    the audit calls for: a ``most recent session'' module (from Session
    review), the needs-a-look flags, and practice conditions for \emph{four}
    windows --- this morning, this afternoon, next morning, next afternoon
    (fixing the doubles gap). Every widget collapsible, and the widget set
    coach-customizable.
  \item \textbf{Upcoming} --- planning, anchored by the Planner (day / week /
    training block) with the Workout planner as its zoomed-in day view.
    Absorbs race plans (from Team board) and the Plan \& Goals material
    currently inside Volume.
  \item \textbf{Team} --- a team landing page (name, picture,
    conference/division information, the team document), with routes into:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Roster} --- the public-roster-style list; selecting an
        athlete opens their page (layout below);
      \item \textbf{Results} --- the central results repository (by meet,
        season progression, record board, courses), where every athlete name
        links to that athlete's page;
      \item \textbf{PRs / season progression} --- selecting an athlete goes
        to their page;
      \item \textbf{Operations} --- logistics: money, equipment, the .ics
        calendar export.
    \end{itemize}
    The athlete page is deliberately \emph{not} a top-level tab: the routes
    converge on it (Figure~\ref{fig:teamflow}), so a coach reaches the same
    athlete object whether they come through the roster, the boards, or a
    result.
  \item \textbf{Recruiting} --- reserved as a tab, but a \emph{v2 product};
    not built in v1. (Recruit-visit mode --- masking athlete names --- ships
    in v1 under Settings.)
  \item \textbf{Settings} --- the standardized menu the Housekeeping audit
    calls for: data importation (Data health), team / coach / program
    settings, the workout planner rules, Parameters and Predictions, bulk
    import/export, recruit-visit mode, and the feature toggles (turn modules
    such as 1-on-1 tracking on or off).
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:proposal} maps the proposal onto the current hub; the expanded
plans below give the per-tab build detail.

\subsection{Expanded design plans}
\label{sec:expanded}

\subsubsection{Today}

The landing page answers ``what do I need to know before practice'' in one
screen. Figure~\ref{fig:todaywire} is the \emph{default} layout --- every slot
on it is a pick from the widget catalog below, so two coaches' Today pages
need not look alike.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Greeting line} --- ``Good morning, Coach. Today is Tuesday,
    September 8.'' Time-of-day aware. The salutation, the name, and what
    follows the date (season day count, days to the next race, nothing) are
    all fields the coach sets.
  \item \textbf{Glance row} --- four small slots. The default four are
    uploaded yesterday, athletes available today, data flags / gone quiet,
    and needs a look; any slot can be swapped for another glance widget ---
    days until the next race, paperwork outstanding, team mileage against
    plan (Table~\ref{tab:glance}).
  \item \textbf{Conditions strip} --- four windows (this morning, this
    afternoon, tomorrow morning, tomorrow afternoon) so doubles are covered.
    The \emph{model} behind workout adjustments is wet-bulb globe
    temperature, but WBGT is a poor thing to show a coach. Each window
    displays temperature, a conditions icon (sun / cloud / rain),
    precipitation chance, and humidity; the WBGT-derived flag surfaces only
    as the window's colour.
  \item \textbf{Today's plan} --- the print option kept one click away.
  \item \textbf{Most recent session} --- the Session review material as a
    module, with historical sessions reachable from it.
  \item \textbf{Tasks} --- the task organizer from Tasks + Links as a widget.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Widgets come in three sizes.} Following the Apple widget model: a
\textbf{glance} (S) widget is one number or flag in a 1$\times$1 tile; a
\textbf{medium} (M) widget is 2$\times$1 and holds a short list or a compact
chart; a \textbf{large} (L) widget is 2$\times$2 and holds a real chart or a
rich panel. The page is a grid the coach arranges --- add, remove, reorder,
and resize where a widget supports two sizes. Some widgets are text, some
are numbers, some are charts; the catalog marks which, and the point of the
chart widgets is that the coach never has to assemble a graph by hand.

\subsubsection{Upcoming}

One planning object at four zoom levels, rather than three separate pages
(Figure~\ref{fig:zoom}): the season block (phases, goals, peak mileage,
down-week depth --- absorbing Volume's Plan \& Goals), the week grid (Week
wall becomes a view option here, not a page), the single day (the Workout
planner's pods, paces, and weather/terrain adjustments), and the athlete
preview (Athlete day as a ``preview'' button, not a destination).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Text-to-structured-workout parsing} is the load-bearing
    technical piece: ``2 mile wu, 5 tempo, 3 cd'' typed in the grid must
    become a structured workout that can be analyzed post hoc.
  \item \textbf{Race plans} move here from Team board and attach to meet
    dates: per-athlete goals plus predicted pace ranges, and eventually MCMC
    field simulations against expected competition.
  \item \textbf{Training paces} (the Pace cards content) surface here as
    links; the paces themselves live on the athlete page.
\end{itemize}

Build order: the four-zoom shell around the existing planner first; the text
parser next; the race-plan predictive layer last.

\paragraph{The athlete page (three tiers, top to bottom).}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=0em,style=nextline,itemsep=3pt]
  \item[Identity (top, glanceable).] Name, gender, class, hometown, MileSplit and Athletic.net links, and PRs --- the public-roster feel the audit of the current Athlete page calls for.
  \item[Secondary.] Recent race results, recent training (a week-over-week mileage graph), attendance / training log, past and current injuries, upcoming registered events (date, name, event), training paces, and stated goals. Person-file material (dietary restrictions, class schedules, paperwork) stays populated further down; anything duplicated elsewhere (e.g.\ 1-on-1 notes) becomes a link, not a repeated widget.
  \item[Advanced analytics (bottom).] The learned attribute models:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Hill Resilience} --- is the runner better on flat or hilly courses? Built from course-elevation learning, MileSplit course rankings, FloRatings A/B/C/D, TullyRunners speed ratings, joshm.dev's Crystal Springs Pace Factor, and the golf-slope-rating analog.
      \item \textbf{Weather Impact} --- how much adverse weather degrades this runner. The constraint: exact per-race time is needed, and getting it at scale from Athletic.net is not viable (see \S\ref{sec:platform}).
      \item Additional analytical models as desired.
    \end{itemize}
\end{description}

Build order: the identity and secondary tiers ship first --- they display
data already held. The analytics tier lands as the platform models mature
(\S\ref{sec:platform}), each model appearing as an optional widget rather
than a blank promise.

\subsubsection{Recruiting (v2)}

Reserved as a tab but not built in v1; recruit-visit mode (masking athlete
names) ships in v1 under Settings. When v2 comes, the tab inherits the
recruiting file from the current person file, and the HS $\leftrightarrow$
college linkage of \S\ref{sec:platform} is its natural engine: a coach looks
up a prospect's verified HS results and course-adjusted marks rather than a
self-reported PR.

\subsubsection{Settings}

One standardized menu (Figure~\ref{fig:settings}) replaces the Housekeeping
tier: data importation from Data health, the Setup panels split into their
own tabs, the Workout planner's ``Rules baked in,'' the Parameters and
Predictions pages, feature toggles (the grand-strategy layering --- run the
product as anything from roster-plus-planner up to the full analytics
surface), recruit-visit mode, and bulk import/export, with ``How this
works'' as the help entry. If the paperwork tracked in Data health is
medical clearances, it moves to the Team tab's roster as a
who-is-missing-paperwork widget --- it is compliance, not data flow. This
tab is a mechanical regrouping with no new functionality, which makes it a
good early milestone.
